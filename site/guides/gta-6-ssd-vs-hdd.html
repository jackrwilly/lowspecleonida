Will GTA 6 need an SSD on PC?

Storage is easy to forget when you're planning a GTA 6 upgrade, but it could matter more than you'd think. Our take: plan on installing GTA 6 on an SSD. A hard drive (HDD) may not cut it.

Heads up: Rockstar has not released official GTA 6 PC system requirements yet. Everything below is an estimate based on how demanding current open-world games are. We will update this page the day official specs drop.

Our estimate

We expect an SSD to be strongly recommended, and possibly required, for GTA 6 on PC. Rockstar hasn't confirmed this, but there are good reasons to expect it:

  • GTA 6 was built for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, which both have fast SSDs built in.
  • A number of recent big PC releases already list an SSD in their minimum or recommended requirements.
  • Open-world games stream the map off your drive constantly as you move through it.

Why a hard drive struggles

A hard drive reads data from a spinning disk, which is much slower than an SSD, especially when a game needs lots of small pieces of data quickly. In open-world games that can mean long loading screens, textures and buildings popping in late, and stutters when you drive or fly fast. Even if the game launches from an HDD, it's likely to be the worst version of the experience.

SATA SSD vs NVMe SSD

There are two common types of SSD:

TypeConnects viaTypical max speed
SATA SSDSATA cable (2.5" drive)~550 MB/s
NVMe SSD (PCIe 3.0)M.2 slot~3,500 MB/s
NVMe SSD (PCIe 4.0)M.2 slotup to ~7,000 MB/s

Any SSD is a huge jump from a hard drive. A SATA SSD is a fine budget option, especially for older PCs without an M.2 slot. If your motherboard has a free M.2 slot, an NVMe drive is usually the better buy and leaves room for faster loading in future games.

How much space to plan for

Rockstar hasn't said how big the PC install will be. Modern open-world games regularly take 100 GB or more, so we'd plan on a 1 TB SSD if you're buying one, rather than trying to squeeze GTA 6 onto a nearly full 500 GB drive. A big hard drive is still fine for storing videos, screenshots and older games.

Quick checks

  • See what drives you have: Task Manager → Performance. Each disk is labeled SSD or HDD.
  • Check your motherboard manual for free M.2 slots and whether they're PCIe 3.0 or 4.0.
  • Install the game on the SSD, not just Windows.

An SSD fits well into any of our budget build tiers, and it pairs nicely with a RAM upgrade (see Is 16GB RAM enough?).

FAQ

Will GTA 6 run on a hard drive at all?

We don't know yet. Rockstar hasn't released PC requirements. Even if it does run, expect longer loads and more stutter.

Do I need a PCIe 4.0 NVMe drive?

Probably not for a good experience. Any SSD should be a big improvement over a hard drive; faster NVMe drives mostly help with load times.

Can I use an external SSD?

A fast external SSD over USB can work for many games, but an internal SSD is the safer bet for performance and reliability.
